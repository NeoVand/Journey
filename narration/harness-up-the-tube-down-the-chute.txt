# Up the tube, down the chute
NORA: Every turn in this room is the same small journey. Up the tube, down the chute, out to a bench, and back into the tray.
THEO: [amused] It sounds like a board game.
NORA: [chuckles] One with very strict rules. Start with the tube. At the start of a turn a copy of the whole tray is loaded into a capsule, and the capsule shoots up to the model. That's the request: one long piece of text.
THEO: And the reply comes down the chute.
NORA: A piece at a time, as it's written. Its words drop onto the pad and slide along the lip into the tray. On the first turn here, the model said: I'll read the scheduler first.
THEO: And then it reads it.
NORA: It asks to. A tool call is still only text, a name and some arguments, so the harness prints it as a card: Read, scheduler.py. The card hops onto the cart, and the cart waits inside the gate to be checked.
THEO: [curious] Where does it go once it's through?
NORA: Along the rail to the benches on the right wall. Each bench is wired through the wall to something real. Read goes to the Files bench, which reaches your files. A pulse runs out along the wire, and back comes the result: two hundred and twelve lines of the scheduler.
THEO: Which goes straight up to the model?
NORA: Not straight away. It rides the cart back and gets tipped into the tray. Then the next turn starts, and the whole tray, result and all, goes up the tube.
THEO: So the model only sees the file on the next trip up.
NORA: Exactly. The second turn ran the test twenty times on the Shell bench. Three failed, seventeen passed. A flaky test, caught in the act.
THEO: [laughs] And the third turn?
NORA: The third reply came down with words and nothing else. Found it: one lock, taken twice. Shall I fix it?
THEO: No card.
NORA: No card, and that's the signal. When a reply asks for no tool, the loop stops and hands the floor back to you.
THEO: [skeptical] So stopping means it's done?
NORA: It means the model stopped asking. Here it stopped to ask you something. A model can also stop, sure it's finished, with the bug still there. Whether the work is really done is for the tests to say, and for you.
THEO: How many trips does a real fix take?
NORA: Five to thirty, usually. And when a reply asks for several calls that don't depend on each other, the harness can run them side by side.
THEO: Up the tube, down the chute, out to a bench, into the tray.
NORA: [warmly] And round again, until a reply asks for nothing.
