# What going back can’t reach
THEO: So I rewound the session to before the bad change, and the commit the agent pushed is still sitting on the remote.
NORA: [short pause] It will be. A rewind puts back files on your disk and nothing else. The push happened out in the world, on the git host, and nothing on your laptop can rewind that.
THEO: But my side says it never happened.
NORA: Your files and your conversation both say so. That's the odd part: after the rewind, your side looks as if the push never happened, and the git host still has the commit. The host doesn't know you went back.
THEO: What else is in that category?
NORA: Anything that reached outside. A message sent. A database changed. A deploy started. Each of those happened in some other system, and your checkpoint never held a copy of any of it. That kind of state can outlive the whole run.
THEO: [skeptical] I can push a revert, though.
NORA: You can, and that's a new action out in the world, on top of the old one. It isn't going back. Put bluntly, nothing undoes a sent message or a merged pull request.
THEO: Do the agents admit this, or is it buried somewhere?
NORA: All three say it plainly. Claude Code's documentation says actions on remote systems, like databases, APIs and deployments, can't be checkpointed. Codex's says changing an instruction doesn't undo an action already completed.
THEO: And Copilot?
NORA: Its documentation says restoring doesn't reverse terminal commands, network requests, deployments or changes to outside services.
THEO: [surprised] Terminal commands too. Even ones it ran on my own laptop?
NORA: Restoring doesn't reverse them. The checkpoint puts files back. What a command did stays done.
THEO: So a rewind is a safety net for my folder, not for the world.
NORA: That's exactly it. And it's why anything that reaches outside goes through the permission gates first. The push, the message, the deploy: those are the calls you want a rule, or you, to look at before they run.
THEO: Because once they've run, there's no going back.
NORA: [warmly] Right. Going back is never a reason to skip the gates. If anything, it's the reason to take them seriously.
