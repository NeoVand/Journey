Concept for the interface chrome of an interactive 3D explainer website, laid over one of its night scenes. The first reference is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile, toy-like 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, with cobalt, amber and jade accents. The second reference is the page as it is today at night: a title at the top left with a small Map button, a row of progress dots with a step counter, a cluster of control buttons at the top right, the card's text on the left, and a framed minimap card at the bottom right. It feels like separate stickers on top of the picture. The third reference is an earlier daytime concept for the same chrome that we liked.

Redesign the chrome so it belongs to the picture, like one artwork:
- Top: one continuous, very thin frosted band across the whole width, quiet and translucent, holding the small title on the left and a few round icon controls on the right (sound, agent picker, back, play, next, the next one in soft amber). The row of progress dots is gone.
- Bottom right: the minimap with no frame and no card: a small aerial view of the whole journey (a winding path with tiny islands and one softly glowing marker where we are), whose edges dissolve into soft drifting cloud and mist on every side. The centre is clear; toward the edges it fades to fully transparent, so it reads as a glimpse of the map through a gap in the clouds far below. A little larger than a thumbnail, never covering the scene's subject.
- Bottom left: a slim context bar, one thin rounded capsule divided into soft coloured segments (cream, tan, pale blue, cobalt, then a run of small amber and jade pieces), with a tiny notch near its far end marking the limit. Quiet, like a progress bar.
- The card text on the left sits straight on the night picture, no box, with a soft shadow.
Behind it, the 3D scene from the second reference, restyled friendlier: frosted milky trays with soft pastel meaning maps and fine coloured threads, warm and calm, a soft out-of-focus server hall far behind. Body text may be soft placeholder lines rather than words; the title may read "Attention".
