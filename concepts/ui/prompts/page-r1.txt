Concept design for the interface of a scroll-driven 3D website that explains how a coding agent works. Keep the 3D scene and its look from the reference screenshot (a sky-blue world with porcelain islands and cream machines), and redesign only the interface around it so the page and the scene feel like one artwork.

1. The top toolbar extends right across the top of the window as one soft frosted-glass band, very light, melting into the sky below it with a gentle fade, holding a small title on the left and a compact row of rounded controls on the right (sound, play, previous, next). No row of progress dots.
2. In the bottom-right corner, a minimap: a slightly larger, live miniature 3D view of the whole loop (islands joined by an orange and a green cable, a blue brain on top), drawn as if seen through a gap in soft clouds, with no hard border at all: its edges feather away into cloud and transparency, more transparent at the outer edges than at the centre, so it blends into the scene behind.
3. Along the bottom edge, a slim horizontal context bar: a softly glowing segmented capsule showing how full the model's context is, its coloured segments (cream, tan, pale blue, cobalt, amber, jade green) filling from the left, about a third full, with a subtle mark for the limit at the right end.
Typography: a rounded geometric display face for headings, a clean humanist sans for body. Sentence case. Keep the card of text on the left side with its heading and two short paragraphs. Elegant, calm, airy, premium. Do not render readable paragraph text: use soft placeholder lines.
