Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website about how a language model reads a sentence. The first reference image is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile, toy-like 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, with cobalt, amber and jade accents, rounded edges, soft studio light. The second reference is an earlier concept for this scene that was beautiful but too fantastical and cold (a cosmic void, glowing filaments, a gold portal). The third is the scene as it is built today, which shows WHAT must be there but looks tacky and plasticky. Make this scene as friendly and warm as the first reference while keeping the second's restraint, delicacy and calm.

The scene: a tall stack of thin rounded trays, evenly spaced, the nearest five or six crisp and the higher ones fading softly into the background. Each tray carries a gentle meaning map: a handful of soft rounded regions in sky blue, mint, peach, butter yellow and lilac, flat and matte, like a picture-book atlas. On the bottom tray, fourteen small cream tiles, like rounded keycaps, one per word of a sentence, each sitting in the region its meaning belongs to. From each tile a fine soft thread climbs up through the trays, most cobalt, some amber. Each thread crosses each tray at a slightly different spot, so you can see a word's meaning drift across the map as it climbs; higher up, related threads lean together into two or three loose bundles over the same region, but every thread stays its own and they never merge into one point.

Materials: the trays are pale frosted glass with softly rounded, polished edges, like milky porcelain-glass; the tiles are cream porcelain with a satin glaze; the threads are fine coloured cords with only a faint inner light.
Setting: a bright, airy sky-blue space like the first reference, with soft daylight, gentle shadows and a pale porcelain floor far below. Threads are coloured and solid, like fine silk cord, not glowing.

No glowing portals, no stars, no sparks, no lens flares, no neon, no thick tubes, no text or letters anywhere. Quietly delightful, clean and immediately readable, like a beautiful exhibit in a science museum that children and engineers both love. Leave the left third of the frame calm and uncluttered for text.
