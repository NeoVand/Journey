Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D website that explains how a transformer language model reads a sentence. Use the reference images for WHAT is shown (a stack of map-like layers, word tiles, threads following each word up the stack), but completely restyle it so it feels refined, clean and premium, like a museum installation or a scientific visualization, never plasticky or tacky.

The scene: a vertical stack of very thin, frosted-glass plates floating in a deep navy space, like the layers of a model, many of them, evenly spaced, the lower ones crisp and the upper ones fading into soft depth. Each plate carries a faint, delicate map of meaning: soft pastel regions printed into the glass like a watercolour atlas. Fourteen tiny glowing beads, one per word of a short sentence, sit on the bottom plate. From each bead, a single hair-thin luminous filament (a worldline) climbs up through the plates, bending a little at each one as the word's meaning moves; the filaments drift together toward one shared warm-gold region near the top as the sentence comes to mean one thing. Colours: deep navy, frosted white glass, soft gold, cobalt, a touch of coral. Subtle depth of field, gentle bloom on the filaments only, no thick tubes, no chunky blocks, no text or letters.

Elegant, airy, quietly beautiful, immediately readable. Leave the left third of the frame calm and dark for text.
