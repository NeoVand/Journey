Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website about how a language model reads a sentence. The first reference image is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile, toy-like 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, with cobalt, amber and jade accents, rounded edges, soft studio light. The other references are our favourite concepts for this scene so far: keep their warmth, calm and friendliness, and refine them.

The scene: one thin rounded tray of pale milky porcelain-glass seen from slightly above, with a second tray below it. Each tray carries a clean meaning map: six or seven soft rounded regions with crisp simple edges in flat pastel colours (sky blue, mint, peach, butter yellow, lilac, soft rose), like a well-designed picture-book atlas, never watercolour blotches. On it sit fourteen small cream porcelain tiles like rounded keycaps, one per word of a sentence, scattered across the map, each in the region its meaning belongs to; four small ones huddle together in one region (the little words). One tile near the end of the sentence, the word being read, stands a little raised with a soft warm amber light inside it. From it, fine matte cords arch back to the tiles of the earlier words, each like a thread pulled into a low bow: its thickness and warmth show how much attention that word gets. Two cords are clearly strongest, thick and warm amber; the rest are hair-thin pale cobalt. The tiles of later words sit grey and unlit under a faint frosted veil, because a word can only look back. From the underside of every tile a fine cord drops through a tiny brass eyelet to the tray below.

Setting: a calm deep-blue room at night, a soft out-of-focus hall of server racks with tiny warm lights far behind, and a warm pool of light on the floor under the stack, so the trays read as real, friendly objects.

No glowing portals, no stars, no sparks, no lens flares, no neon, no glowing lines, no thick tubes, no text or letters anywhere. Quietly delightful, clean, precise and immediately readable, like a beautiful exhibit in a science museum that children and engineers both love. Leave the left third of the frame calm and uncluttered for text.
