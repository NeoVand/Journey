Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website about how a language model reads a sentence. The first reference image is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile, toy-like 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, with cobalt, amber and jade accents, rounded edges, soft studio light. The other references are our favourite concepts for this scene so far: keep their warmth, calm and friendliness, and refine them.

The scene: a tall stack of thin rounded trays, evenly spaced, the nearest five crisp and the higher ones fading softly upward. The trays are thick cream paper-board with softly rounded corners, the maps screen-printed on them. Each tray carries a clean meaning map: six or seven soft rounded regions with crisp simple edges in flat pastel colours (sky blue, mint, peach, butter yellow, lilac, soft rose), like a well-designed picture-book atlas, never watercolour blotches. On the bottom tray sit fourteen small cream porcelain tiles like rounded keycaps, one per word of a sentence, scattered across the map, each in the region its meaning belongs to; four small ones huddle together in one region (the little words). From each tile one fine matte cord, like cotton thread (most cobalt, some amber), climbs up and passes through every tray at a small neat hole, the spot where that word sits on that layer; between trays the cord bends gently toward its next spot, so you can follow each word's meaning drifting across the maps as it climbs. Higher up, related cords lean together over the same region, but each stays its own.

Setting: a calm deep-blue room at night, a soft out-of-focus hall of server racks with tiny warm lights far behind, and a warm pool of light on the floor under the stack, so the trays read as real, friendly objects.

No glowing portals, no stars, no sparks, no lens flares, no neon, no glowing lines, no thick tubes, no text or letters anywhere. Quietly delightful, clean, precise and immediately readable, like a beautiful exhibit in a science museum that children and engineers both love. Leave the left third of the frame calm and uncluttered for text.
