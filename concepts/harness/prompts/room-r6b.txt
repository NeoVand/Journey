Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website: the inside of a coding agent's harness, the program on your laptop that runs the agent, caught in the middle of one turn of its loop. The first reference is the agreed art direction for the whole site; the second is today's version of this room (keep its cut-away cream room on a porcelain base and its three-quarter view from above; lose its scattered boxes); the third is the closest earlier concept; the fourth is the hero object. Materials: satin porcelain, cream keycap tiles, small brass fittings, matte soft cables, frosted glass. Warm soft daylight, gentle contact shadows, calm. Friendly and precise, like a beautifully made porcelain-and-brass toy: no glowing tubes, no robot arms, no hazard stripes, no chevrons, no screens full of glow.

The room has low walls (so the things outside it are visible beyond them) and fills the right two-thirds of the frame. The left third of the frame is calm, empty sky-blue for a text card.

THE HERO, across the middle of the floor from left to right: the context, everything the model reads this turn. A very long, low porcelain tray with a fine ruler of tick marks along its front rim and a firm brass end stop at its far right end (the most it can ever hold). From its LEFT end it is packed edge to edge with rounded keycap tiles in coloured runs, in this order: a run of caramel-tan tiles (the tools the agent may ask for), a run of pale wheat tiles (its instructions and your rules), two pale sky-blue tiles (facts about your machine), one cobalt-blue tile (your prompt), then many repeating little groups, each one amber tile (a tool request), a run of jade-green tiles of varying length (what the tool returned) and one pale grey-blue tile (the model's words). The packed part reaches about a third of the way; the rest is empty. A frosted glass cover lies over all the packed tiles except the newest group (what the provider keeps cached from last turn). Parked at the far right end, by the end stop, a small porcelain press on wheels that ride the tray's rims.

ONE TURN IN MOTION, around the tray, each station a single large, simple shape:
- Back wall, left: four drawers set into the wall, slightly open, holding tiles of their colours (caramel, wheat, sky blue, cobalt). Beside them a small shelf of little folded cards (skills).
- Back right corner: a round brass hatch at the top of the wall with a clear glass tube rising out of the room into the sky. In the tube a porcelain capsule rises, carrying a small COPY of the whole packed tray: the request goes up to the model. The tray itself stays where it is, full.
- Left side, near the back: a slim glass chute coming down from the sky into the room, and down it falls a ribbon of new tiles: pale grey-blue word tiles and one bigger amber card (a tool request).
- Just outside the left wall, a laptop on the porcelain base, screen toward the room, a few grey-blue tiles going to it through a slot: the words you see.
- Front, centre: a slim porcelain arch, the gate, with a small dome beacon on top, three small lamps with the green one lit, a little brass bell, a round dial on its post and one soft cable plugged into its side. An amber card is just passing through it.
- Front right: three round brass ports in the low wall with soft cables running out of the room to a paper folder, a small terminal and a tiny server box further away. At one port an amber card goes in, and a short run of jade tiles comes back out.
- A jade tile being set into the tray's first empty slot: the result goes back in, and the loop goes round again.
- Right wall: a slot through which a long ribbon of thin cards runs out to a small porcelain filing cabinet outside the room.
- Front left corner: a small cut-away room-in-a-room with its own tiny tray.
- One soft groove in the floor joins the stations in order: chute, gate, ports, tray, hatch. A small amber puck travels in it, now at the gate.

No text, letters or numbers anywhere. No people. Every object means something; the whole room reads at a glance; the tray is the largest, brightest, most detailed thing.
