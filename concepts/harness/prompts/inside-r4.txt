Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website that shows, accurately, the inside of a coding agent's harness: the program on your laptop that runs the agent. The first reference is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, cobalt, amber and jade accents, rounded edges, soft studio light, sky-blue world. The second reference is our best concept so far for this room; keep its idea and layout, but make it calmer, more refined and less like a plastic toy: fewer, larger, simpler parts; milky frosted glass, satin porcelain, soft brushed metal; no hazard stripes, no chevron arrows, no glowing tubes.

THE CENTRE is the context, the one thing the model will read: a tall milky-glass column holding a neat stack of flat porcelain layers. From the bottom up, in the order the model reads them: a tan layer (the tools it may ask for), a thick cream layer (its instructions), a thin pale-blue layer (facts about your machine), a thin cobalt layer (your prompt), then thin alternating amber layers (the model's tool requests) and jade layers (the results). The lower part of the stack, everything that has not changed since the last turn, is wrapped in a soft frosted sleeve: the part the provider keeps cached. A slim brass ruler on the column's side with a small red notch near the top: the limit of what fits.

AROUND it, one smooth ring track with only four stations, clockwise: a capsule carrying a copy of the stack up through the ceiling (send); a ribbon of small tiles coming back down (stream); a slim arch that checks the amber request card, with a small socket where your own rule plugs in and three small lamps (check); a pair of soft robotic arms reaching through round ports in the wall to your files, a terminal and a small extra tool server outside the room (run), while a new jade layer is set on top of the stack. Beside the column, a small porcelain filing cabinet receives a thin copy of every layer: the transcript saved on disk.

No text or letters anywhere. No people. Every object stands for something real; the whole room reads at one glance. Three-quarter view from above, a cut-away cream room. Leave the left third of the frame calm for text.
