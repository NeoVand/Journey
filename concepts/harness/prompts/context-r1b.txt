Concept art for a hero object in an interactive 3D explainer website about how a coding agent works: the context, the one thing the model reads each turn, drawn as a real object. The first reference is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, cobalt, amber and jade accents, rounded edges, soft studio light, sky-blue world. The second is the room inside the harness where this object lives; the third shows a slim context bar in the page that this object must echo. Calm, refined, satin and milky-glass materials rather than shiny plastic.

The object: one long, low porcelain tray with a fixed length, like a ruler-length rail, with a firm end stop at its far end: the limit of what fits. Into it, from the near end, small rounded tiles are packed edge to edge in coloured runs, in the order the model reads them: tan (the tools it may ask for), cream and thick (its instructions), thin pale blue (facts about your machine), thin cobalt (your prompt), then thin alternating amber (the model's tool requests) and jade (their results), turn after turn. Each run's length is its size. The first long stretch, everything unchanged since the last turn, sits under a frosted glass cover: what the provider keeps cached. A soft porcelain arm is placing one more jade tile at the end of the line; the empty stretch before the end stop is clearly shorter than the full part. Floating on a small porcelain island in the sky-blue world, three-quarter view, product-shot clarity.

No text or letters anywhere. No people. Nothing decorative: every part means something. Leave the left third of the frame calm for text.
