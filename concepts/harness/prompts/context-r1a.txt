Concept art for a hero object in an interactive 3D explainer website about how a coding agent works: the context, the one thing the model reads each turn, drawn as a real object. The first reference is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, cobalt, amber and jade accents, rounded edges, soft studio light, sky-blue world. The second is the room inside the harness where this object lives; the third shows a slim context bar in the page that this object must echo. Calm, refined, satin and milky-glass materials rather than shiny plastic.

The object: a tall milky-glass column on a small porcelain base, holding a neat stack of flat porcelain layers, from the bottom up in the order the model reads them: tan (the tools it may ask for), cream and thick (its instructions), thin pale blue (facts about your machine), thin cobalt (your prompt), then thin alternating amber (the model's tool requests) and jade (their results), turn after turn. Each layer's thickness is its size. The lower part, everything unchanged since the last turn, sits inside a soft frosted sleeve: what the provider keeps cached. A slim brass ruler stands beside the column with a small red notch near the top: the limit of what fits. A soft porcelain arm is setting one new jade layer on top. Floating on a small porcelain island in the sky-blue world, three-quarter view, product-shot clarity.

No text or letters anywhere. No people. Nothing decorative: every part means something. Leave the left third of the frame calm for text.
