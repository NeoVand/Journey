Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website: the inside of a coding agent's harness, the program on your laptop that runs the agent. The first reference is the agreed art direction for the whole site; the second is today's version of this room (keep its cut-away cream room and its loop idea, lose its scattered boxes); the third is the object at the heart of the new design; the fourth shows the friendlier materials we want: satin porcelain, cream keycap tiles, matte cords, small brass fittings. Daylight, soft studio light, gentle contact shadows, calm and warm. Friendly and precise, like a beautifully made wooden-and-porcelain toy, not a factory: no glowing tubes, no robot arms, no hazard stripes, no chevrons, no screens full of glow.

THE HERO, filling the middle of the room's floor: the context, the one thing the model reads each turn. A long, low porcelain tray of fixed length with a firm end stop at its far right end (the limit of what fits). Packed edge to edge from its left end, rounded keycap tiles in coloured runs, each run's length its size, in this order: a run of pale wheat tiles (the agent's instructions), a few pale sky-blue tiles (facts about your machine), a long run of caramel-tan tiles (the tools it may ask for, the biggest part), one cobalt tile (your prompt), then repeating little groups of one amber tile (a tool request), a longer jade-green run (its result) and one pale grey-blue tile (the model's own words). A frosted glass cover lies over all the tiles except the newest group: what the provider keeps cached. About a third of the tray, before the end stop, is still empty.

AROUND THE TRAY, five stations, one for each step of the loop, each a single large, simple shape, joined by a soft dashed path on the floor that runs clockwise round the tray:
1. At the tray's left end, four drawers set into the back wall, each pulled out a little and holding tiles of its colour (wheat, sky blue, caramel, cobalt): the harness packs the tray from them.
2. Behind the tray, a round brass hatch in the ceiling: a small porcelain capsule rises through it carrying a copy of the whole tray (sent to the model, far away).
3. Beside it, a slim chute from the ceiling lets a ribbon of new tiles drop back down (the reply streaming in): grey-blue word tiles, and one amber card that is a request, not an action.
4. To the right, a slim porcelain arch with three small lamps (green, amber, red) and a little brass bell on top: the amber request must pass the reader's own rules here, and the bell means "ask the person first".
5. In the front right corner, three round ports in the low wall with soft cables running out of the room to a paper folder, a small terminal and a tiny server outside: the harness does the work on your machine. A new jade tile is being set into the tray's open end: the result goes back in, and the loop goes round again.
In the front left corner, a small porcelain filing cabinet with an open drawer full of thin cards: everything is written down on disk.

No text, letters or numbers anywhere. No people. Every object means something; the whole room reads at a glance. Three-quarter view from above, cut-away cream room on a porcelain base, sky-blue world beyond. Keep the left third of the frame calm and low for a text card.
