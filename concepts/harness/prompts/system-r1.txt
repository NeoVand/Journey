Concept art for one overview scene of an interactive 3D explainer website about how a coding agent works, answering one question: what runs where? The first reference is the art direction agreed for the whole site: friendly, tactile, toy-like 3D objects in soft porcelain, cream and pastel, cobalt, amber and jade accents, rounded edges, soft daylight, floating porcelain islands in a sky-blue world. The second reference is the harness cabinet as it is built today.

A calm diorama of three floating islands, seen from above at three-quarters, linked by fine cables and one glass tube:
- YOUR LAPTOP island (left, nearest): a laptop on a small desk; beside it the harness cabinet, cut open so we see the small milky-glass context column and the little ring track of its loop inside. From the cabinet, short cables reach a drawer of files and a small terminal screen on the same island: the tools run here, on your machine.
- A SMALL island to one side: an extra tool server, linked to the harness by one cable.
- THE PROVIDER island (right, larger and farther away): a clean porcelain building. The glass tube from the harness arrives at its front door, where a capsule passes in order through: a gate that reads a key card (your account), two quiet inspection arches (safety classifiers that check what goes in), a small machine that cuts text into little tiles (the tokenizer), and then a hall of server racks with the model inside, a softly glowing core. On the way back out, the reply passes another inspection arch before it streams back along the tube as a ribbon of small tiles.
Everything outside the laptop island is visibly far away; the loop and the tools are visibly on the laptop. No text or letters anywhere. No people. Spacious, clear, immediately readable, like a beautiful museum model.
