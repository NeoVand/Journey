Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website: the inside of a coding agent's harness, the program on your laptop that runs the agent, caught mid-turn, seen from high above so the whole floor plan reads. References: (1) the agreed art direction for the whole site; (2) today's version of this room in the site: keep its room and materials (a cut-away cream room on a porcelain base, tall back and side walls, open at the front), but look down from much higher: about 65 degrees above, from the front, the whole room centred and filling the frame, so every station and the floor plan are clear; (3) the latest concept of the objects and materials: satin porcelain, cream keycap tiles, brass fittings, matte soft cables, frosted glass; (4) THE FLOOR PLAN, seen from above: follow its placement exactly, but not its drawing style, and draw none of its words.

The room has three zones, one per thing the harness does, each with a barely tinted floor:
REMEMBERS, along the back (faint warm wheat floor): a long, low porcelain tray runs along the back wall from left of centre almost to the right corner: the context. Rounded keycap tiles packed edge to edge from its left end in coloured runs: caramel-tan (tools), pale wheat (instructions), pale sky blue (your machine), one cobalt (your prompt), then little groups of amber, jade-green and pale grey-blue; the packed part reaches about a third of the way, the rest of the tray empty with a ruler of tick marks along its front edge. A frosted glass cover over all but the newest tiles. A brass end stop at the tray's right end, backed by a small porcelain ram housing (the press). A narrow rail (the lip) along the tray's front. In the back wall above the tray's left end, a column of four drawers set into the wall, one above the other, slightly open; to the right on the back wall, a small shelf with a row of closed cards; further right, a small dark slot in the back wall where a thin paper ribbon goes in, the ribbon running along the tray's back edge from a small writer head that sits at the end of the packed tiles.
THE LOOP, in the two back corners: in the back-RIGHT corner a tall standing glass tube from the floor up out of the top of the room, a porcelain capsule rising in it carrying a small copy of the packed tray. In the back-LEFT corner a slim glass chute coming down from above along the left wall, pale grey-blue word tiles and one amber card falling down it onto a small porcelain pad at the tray's left end.
CHECKS, on the left by the reader (faint blue floor): just in front of the pad, against the left wall, the gate: a slim porcelain arch with a small dome beacon, three small lamps (green lit), a little brass bell, a round dial and a slim rack of three small cards. In the left wall beside it a wide window, facing the gate, through which a laptop screen is seen; just forward of the window a brass port in the wall with one soft cable to the gate.
ACTS, on the right by your machine (faint green floor): three small porcelain workbenches in a row along the right wall, one behind the other, each with a small board facing the front, each wired by a short soft cable into a brass port in the right wall. OUTSIDE the right wall, on a narrow porcelain ledge, clearly visible: a paper folder with a little stack of copies, a small terminal inside a glass fence, and a small server box, each straight outside its bench. In the front-right corner, inside the room, a small cut-away miniature of this whole room (its own tiny tray, tube, chute and gate): a helper.
A thin brass rail on the floor runs from the gate along in front of the tray to the right, then turns toward the front along the benches to the miniature room. On it a small amber cart carries an amber card toward a bench. The front-left and centre of the floor are calm and open.

No text, letters or numbers anywhere. No people. Warm soft daylight, gentle contact shadows, no glow, no robot arms, no hazard stripes. Every object means something; the room reads at a glance; the tray is the largest, brightest, most detailed thing.
