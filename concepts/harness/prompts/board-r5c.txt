Concept art for an interactive 3D explainer website about how a coding agent works: the whole inside of the harness (the program on your laptop that runs the agent) shown as one round porcelain board, like a beautifully made board game seen from high above at a steep angle, so that its loop reads at a single glance. The first reference is the agreed art direction; the second is this room today (keep its loop, lose its scattered boxes); the third is the object at its heart. Friendly and calm: satin porcelain, cream keycap tiles, matte soft cords, small brass fittings, soft daylight, gentle contact shadows. No glowing tubes, no robot arms, no hazard stripes, no chevrons.

Across the middle of the board lies the context: a long, low porcelain tray with a firm end stop at its right end (the limit of what fits), packed from its left end with rounded keycap tiles in coloured runs, each run's length its size, in order: pale wheat (the instructions), a few pale sky-blue (facts about your machine), a long caramel-tan run (the tools, the biggest part), one cobalt (your prompt), then two or three repeating groups of one amber tile (a tool request), a jade-green run (its result) and one pale grey-blue tile (the model's words). A frosted glass cover over all but the newest group (cached). A third of the tray still empty.

Round the tray, a single soft groove circles the board clockwise, with five round stations sitting on it like the spaces of a board game, each one a single simple object: four little drawers holding tiles of the first four colours (pack); a small porcelain capsule on a short brass mast pointing up out of the board (send to the model); a little chute with a ribbon of grey-blue tiles and one amber card falling from it (the reply); a slim arch with three lamps, green, amber and red, and a tiny brass bell (check against your rules, ask if needed); three small round ports with soft cords running off the board's edge to a paper folder, a little terminal and a tiny server (run on your machine). A jade tile sits between the last station and the tray's open end, about to go in. Beside the board, a small porcelain filing drawer keeps a copy of every tile.

No text, letters or numbers anywhere. No people. Every object means something. The board floats on a small porcelain island in a sky-blue world with soft clouds. Keep the left third of the frame calm for a text card.
