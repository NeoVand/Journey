Concept art for one card of an interactive 3D explainer website, a close-up in the room shown in the second reference (same materials, light and style; the first reference is the site's agreed art direction). Long into a session: the long porcelain tray is packed all the way to its brass end stop. From its left end: a short run of caramel-tan tiles (the tools), pale wheat tiles (instructions and your rules), two pale sky-blue tiles (your machine), one cobalt tile (your prompt), then a very long stretch of many little groups of amber, jade-green and grey-blue tiles: the conversation, turn after turn.

The moment: compaction. The small porcelain press on wheels rides the tray's rims from the right and rolls over the conversation, and where it has passed, all those amber, jade and grey-blue tiles have been squeezed into one single long orange block (a summary), sitting right after the cobalt tile, with the rest of the tray empty again. The caramel, wheat, sky-blue and cobalt tiles at the start are untouched: the press stops before them. Meanwhile, through a slot in the right wall, a long ribbon of thin cards still runs out to a small porcelain filing cabinet outside the room, carrying a copy of every tile ever placed: the record keeps everything, even what the tray let go. Beside the cabinet, a neat stack of thin folders.

Close three-quarter view from above along the tray, the press in the middle, the filing cabinet at the right edge; calm warm daylight, satin porcelain and brass, no glow. The left third of the frame calm and soft for a text card. No text, letters or numbers anywhere. No people.
