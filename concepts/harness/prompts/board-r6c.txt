Concept art for one scene of an interactive 3D explainer website: the inside of a coding agent's harness, the program on your laptop that runs the agent, shown as one round porcelain board, caught in the middle of one turn of its loop. The first reference is the agreed art direction for the whole site; the second is the closest earlier concept (keep its round board and its clarity); the third is the hero object. Materials: satin porcelain, cream keycap tiles, small brass fittings, matte soft cables, frosted glass. Warm soft daylight, gentle contact shadows, calm, a few soft clouds. Friendly and precise, like a beautifully made porcelain-and-brass board game: no glowing tubes, no robot arms, no hazard stripes, no chevrons.

The board fills the right two-thirds of the frame, seen three-quarters from above. The left third of the frame is calm, empty sky-blue for a text card.

THE HERO, straight across the board's middle from left to right: the context, everything the model reads this turn. A long, low porcelain tray with a fine ruler of tick marks along its front rim and a firm brass end stop at its right end (the most it can ever hold). From its LEFT end it is packed edge to edge with rounded keycap tiles in coloured runs, in this order: caramel-tan tiles (the tools), pale wheat tiles (instructions and your rules), two pale sky-blue tiles (facts about your machine), one cobalt-blue tile (your prompt), then repeating little groups of one amber tile (a tool request), a run of jade-green tiles (the result) and one pale grey-blue tile (the model's words). The packed part reaches about a third of the way; the rest is empty. A frosted glass cover over all but the newest group. A small porcelain press on wheels parked by the end stop.

Round the board's rim, one groove: the loop. On it, clockwise, each station one large simple shape on a little round plinth:
- Back left: four drawers in a small porcelain chest, slightly open, holding caramel, wheat, sky-blue and cobalt tiles; a little shelf of folded cards beside it (skills).
- Back right: a clear glass tube rising from a brass ring up into the sky; a porcelain capsule rises in it carrying a small COPY of the packed tray (the request goes up to the model). The tray itself stays.
- Front right, at the rim: three round brass ports with soft cables running off the board to a paper folder, a small terminal and a tiny server box, each on its own little cloud-island outside.
- Front centre: a slim porcelain arch, the gate, with a small dome beacon, three small lamps (green lit), a little brass bell, a dial on its post and one soft cable plugged into its side; an amber card passing through.
- Front left: a slim glass chute coming down from the sky onto the rim, a ribbon of grey-blue word tiles and one amber card falling down it (the reply). Beside it, off the board on its own cloud-island, a laptop: your screen.
- A jade tile being set into the tray's first empty slot; a small amber puck in the groove marks where the turn is.
- Off the board on the right, a small porcelain filing cabinet on a cloud-island, joined by a ribbon of thin cards: everything is written down. Off the board on the left, a tiny second board with its own tiny tray: a helper agent's loop.

No text, letters or numbers anywhere. No people. Every object means something; it reads at a glance; the tray is the largest, brightest, most detailed thing.
