- Five-second: verbs beat nouns, but **"it asks" collides with card 4**, whose title "Asking is not doing" uses
  *asking* for the model's request. The reader would meet "the harness asks" and "the model asks" for opposite things.
- Facts: "the room *around* it" puts the model inside the room; it is upstairs, far away. And the gate mostly
  doesn't ask anyone: your rules decide most calls, and you only when they say so.
- One object: fine; the shelf and press earn their places through cards 3 and 5.
- World: keeping the tall right wall is right (the user asked for the same room).
- Camera: still unsolved. Benches against a wall seen at 19° read as furniture, not as "your machine".
- Cards and delight: good; "the model never moves; the harness runs" is the line to keep.

## The description, in one breath

> The model, far away, can only read and write, and it forgets everything between turns. The harness is the
> program on your machine that does the rest. **It remembers**: one tray holds everything so far, and every turn a
> copy goes up. **It checks**: when the reply asks for something, your rules decide, or you do. **It acts**: it does
> it on your machine and puts what happened in the tray. Round and round, until the model asks for nothing.

The pairing carries it: **the model asks; the harness checks, then acts.** The three verbs come in the order a turn
visits them, and each is one thing to point at: the tray, the gate, the benches.

## One turn

As round 2: copy up on the right, reply down on the left, words along the lip and out of your window, the cart takes
an amber card to the gate (green, ask you, or red: a red tile goes in and nothing runs), on to a bench, and back to
tip the result in at the open end. The cart stopping at the open end is the end of the turn.

## What changed from round 2, and why

- **"Checks", not "asks"**: true (rules decide most calls), and it leaves *asking* to the model, as card 4 needs.
- **"The program on your machine"**, not "the room around it": the model is not in the room.
- **The camera, measured, not guessed.** The wide shot (az 16°, el 38°, a 22° lens) stands about 41 units away
  (from `rig.ts`: the distance that fits the room in the frame), at about x 8.9, z 30.6: *just right of the right
  wall's line*. So from the camera the right wall is almost edge-on (about 3°, worse than either of us said), the
  left wall is seen at about 27°, and the back wall and floor carry nearly all the reading. But it also means
  **what stands just outside the right wall, toward the front, is in plain view**, because the camera is on that
  side. So: the benches stand on the floor by the right wall with their boards facing the camera, each wired through
  the wall to the real thing just outside it (your files, your shell inside its fence when sandboxed, an MCP server),
  in the right wall's front half. Same room, same tall walls, no camera change, and "on your machine" is something
  you see. (A camera swung left of centre would only trade walls: at −10° the left wall and its window go edge-on.)
- **One more delight**, meaningful: each port's board lights when its bench runs, so in card 2 you watch the act
  leave the room through the wall.
